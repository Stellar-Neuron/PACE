PACE: Policy-Native Adaptive Decision Timing for Long-Horizon Reasoning

Chen Li, Zhantao Yang, Fangyi Chen, Han Zhang, Anudeepsekhar Bolimera, Marios Savvides
Carnegie Mellon University
arXiv Paper Project Page

Abstract

Long-horizon reasoning requires deciding not only what actions to take, but how many to execute open-loop before replanning. This number—the execution depth—balances replanning cost against compounding execution errors. Most current systems either fix the execution depth as a hand-tuned scalar or adjust it at inference time with heuristic rules decoupled from the policy; we argue both can be suboptimal. In this work, we treat the execution depth as a learnable, history-conditioned variable of the policy itself, and propose PACE, a model-native VLM policy that jointly predicts what to execute and for how long under a hard decision budget. We evaluate PACE on four long-horizon environments spanning fully and partially observable settings and visual and textual modalities: Sliding Puzzle, Sokoban, ALFWorld, and ScienceWorld. Across all settings, PACE improves success rate by 3.1 ~ 15.6 percentage points, while using fewer decisions on average, achieving empirical success–decision Pareto dominance.

Contributions

  • (C1) Problem formulation. We formulate execution-depth selection as a decision process under a hard budget, with actions executed open-loop between observations. With the action-generation mechanism fixed, we establish a sufficient condition for a history-conditioned depth policy to strictly outperform the best fixed depth in the same admissible set.
  • (C2) Unified adaptive policy and infrastructure. We propose a single policy-native VLM with a depth head and a compact action head sharing one backbone, trained jointly under one objective, along with our self-built vision–language puzzle environment and an automatic macro-action data pipeline.
  • (C3) Success–decision Pareto dominance. Across seven settings in four environments, PACE improves success by 3.1–15.6 percentage points with fewer decisions than the best-performing fixed depth under the hard budget.

Method

PACE architecture. The backbone representation zt feeds a depth head and an autoregressive action decoder. The decoder generates eight actions independently of the selected depth ht; the first ht execute without intermediate feedback. RL jointly updates backbone LoRA and both heads.

  • A lightweight depth head and a compact action decoder share a single 7B vision–language backbone.
  • The depth head predicts a distribution over ℋ = {1, 2, 4, 8}.
  • The decoder generates up to hmax = 8 actions, of which the first ht are executed open-loop.
  • The two heads are trained jointly: first an SFT warm start on counterfactual macro-action data, then a GRPO-style RL objective under the hard budget K.
  • Each decision consumes one unit of a hard budget K, regardless of depth; policy inputs omit budget and step counters.

Ablation Study

Budget Generalization without Retraining

Without retraining or changes to the input format, PACE exceeds the same-checkpoint hindsight-best fixed depth by 4.7–15.0 percentage points across all seven larger-budget settings.

Analysis

State-conditioned depth under matched training and evaluation budgets. In both puzzles, mean selected depth decreases from near-goal to far-from-goal states. Both puzzles exhibit a near-deep/far-shallow pattern.

Execution reliability and depth–success frontiers. Longer execution saves decisions but reduces puzzle optimal-path reliability below p̂(1)h at larger depths. In text domains, no tested fixed depth matches PACE's success on the same checkpoint.

Selected execution-depth distributions across seven settings. PACE uses all four depths in every setting. On ScienceWorld medium, it selects h=8 in 82.9% of decisions yet exceeds fixed-8 success by 20 percentage points.

Action and depth entropy during RL rollouts. Neither mean action nor mean depth entropy approaches zero over the displayed interval.
